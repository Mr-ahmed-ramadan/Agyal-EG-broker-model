Investor business plan

The long-form plan behind the investor pack: the opportunity, the economics, the path from one broker to many, what we are raising and what it buys. Figures are illustrative of the model at the stated assumptions, not a forecast.

The case in one line. Agyal earns 1% a year on every pound of retail money it puts into Egyptian government paper, through brokers who already hold the licences and the clients — and the company reaches break-even on a single broker, so capital buys growth rather than survival.

1. Why this exists

Egyptian retail savers hold most of their savings in bank deposits. Government treasury bills and bonds frequently pay more than a same-term deposit, but retail access runs through bank branches with high minimums and manual paperwork — so the better instrument stays out of reach for ordinary savers.

Brokerage firms already hold the licences and the client relationships needed to close that gap. What they lack is the technology: a digital onboarding flow, a connection to bank liquidity, a ledger, and a client app. Building it is slow and expensive, which is why most have not.

We built it so they do not have to — and we are paid out of the trades it enables.

2. The model

A white-label platform. The broker's brand faces the client; the broker keeps the licence, holds all client money in its own segregated account, and owns the books and records. Agyal provides the software and is paid by the broker — never by the end client, never out of client money.

For an investor the consequences are the ones that matter:

  • No regulatory licence required on our side — the business is structured so the licensed party is the broker. We are seeking the FRA's confirmation of exactly this.
  • No client money or client assets on our balance sheet, so no client-money risk, no capital adequacy burden, and a clean balance sheet.
  • No retail acquisition cost. We sell to brokers; they bring the clients. There is no paid-marketing line in this plan.
  • Each additional broker is configuration, not a new build. The platform is multi-tenant by design, with per-tenant isolation enforced in the database itself.

3. Unit economics

The margin sits inside the yield and is disclosed to the client. Platform defaults as configured in the product today:

ComponentBasis pointsTo whom
Custody and operations5Custodian / operations
Broker margin50The licensed broker
Platform margin100Agyal
Total deduction from gross yield155Against a 300 bps cap enforced in software
Agyal revenue ≈ assets on platform × 1.00% × time held. Every EGP 1 million held for a year earns roughly EGP 10,000 — and the client still beats a same-term deposit. Revenue recurs as clients roll maturing paper, so a client acquired once pays for as long as they stay invested.

The revenue line behaves like an asset-based fee rather than transactional brokerage: it compounds with balances rather than depending on trading frequency. Margins are agreed per broker and will compress as brokers gain leverage — section 7 models that rather than ignoring it.

4. Go-to-market

  • Win brokers, not clients. Each firm brings an already-licensed client base and its own brand.
  • Branded demo in a day. The admin console spins up a prospect demo with the broker's logo and colours; materials go out as tracked links.
  • Pilot in 8–12 weeks — one or two partner banks over FIX, the broker's client-money and custody accounts, an invited first group of clients.
  • Then expand — more banks and instruments per broker, then more brokers on the same platform.

Being candid about what is hard: reaching and signing brokers is the binding constraint on this business, not building the product. The plan treats broker acquisition as the primary activity and the main use of any capital raised.

6. Why bootstrapped-to-break-even matters

The company is founder-run with no external funding, no employees, no office and no debt. Total operating cost is about USD 1,000 a month — USD 12,000 a year — and the founder takes no draw.

Because revenue is roughly 1% of assets per year, break-even assets equal one hundred times annual cost:

Break-even ≈ USD 1.2 million of client assets — roughly EGP 55–66 million depending on the rate, or about 550–660 clients holding EGP 100,000 each.

Set against the Pilot stage above — one broker, 500 clients, EGP 50m of assets — the business reaches break-even at roughly the first broker relationship working properly.

The investment consequence: capital is not required to keep this business alive. It buys speed — more brokers signed per year, and the operational depth to support them. An investor is funding growth, not runway, and the downside case is a slower business rather than a dead one. A year in which no broker signs costs roughly USD 12,000, not a round.

Said plainly, because it cuts both ways: a cost base this small is evidence of discipline, not of scale. It also means the company has been constrained in what it could spend on reaching brokers — which is precisely what the raise is for.

10. What exists today

  • A functionally complete platform: four web apps (client, broker console, admin console, landing), a FIX 4.4 gateway with transactional outbox and inbox, and a double-entry ledger whose trial balance must net to zero.
  • Full client lifecycle working end to end: onboarding with eKYC and AML screening, unified-code and custody linkage, deposits, buying and selling over FIX, settlement, coupons and maturity with tax withholding, and withdrawals with maker-checker approval.
  • Multi-tenant from the ground up, with isolation enforced by PostgreSQL row-level security — each additional broker is configuration.
  • Automated unit tests plus an end-to-end trading test that exercises the whole flow against real processes.
  • A live hosted demo with simulated banks, and the ability to produce a branded demo for a prospect in a day.

Not yet live: partner-bank FIX, eKYC, AML screening, MCDR custody confirmations and SMS all run against simulators today. We state this plainly here for the same reason we state it to the regulator — the plan is better served by an accurate picture than a flattering one.

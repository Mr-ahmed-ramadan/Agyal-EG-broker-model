Business plan

The business, how it earns, what it costs to run, and when it covers its own costs. Prepared to accompany an application to the FRA Sandbox for Regulatory Guidance. Projections are projections; every assumption behind them is listed so it can be challenged on its merits.

The plan in one line. Agyal sells software to FRA-licensed brokerage firms and is paid a margin on the trades that software enables. The company is founder-run and bootstrapped, with a cost base small enough that a single modest broker covers it — so the business does not depend on raising money, and is under no pressure to grow faster than it can be governed.

1. Executive summary

  • What we sell. A white-label, FIX-native platform that lets a licensed brokerage firm offer retail investors digital access to Egyptian government treasury bills and bonds under the broker's own brand.
  • Who pays. The broker, under a software and services agreement. Never the end client, and never out of client money.
  • How we earn. A margin of 100 basis points built into the yield, booked to a separate payable account in the broker's ledger and settled monthly against an invoice.
  • Cost base. Founder-run and bootstrapped. Running costs are hosting and per-use service fees; they scale with usage rather than stepping up ahead of it.
  • Break-even. Because revenue is approximately 1% of assets on the platform per year, break-even is reached at roughly one hundred times annual cost in assets — a level one modest broker relationship can reach. Section 7 shows this across a range of cost levels.
  • Funding. No external funding is required to operate, nor to meet the obligations of a guided phase. Growth capital would accelerate broker onboarding, not keep the lights on.
  • Principal dependency. Signing licensed brokers. This is a commercial risk, not a technical one, and is treated as the central risk of the plan.

2. The business and the model

Agyal is a technology provider, not a regulated intermediary. The licensed broker is the counterparty to every client and every trade, holds all client money in its own segregated account, and owns the books and records. Agyal provides the software, the onboarding workflow, the multi-bank execution gateway and the ledger the broker operates.

ConcernOwner

This division is not merely contractual. Every money-moving function in the platform is restricted to the broker's own finance and operations roles; the platform-operator role has no access to any of them. The consequence for this plan is that Agyal carries no client-money risk and holds no client assets on its balance sheet — the company's balance sheet is its own cash and its own software.

3. Market and opportunity

Egyptian retail savers hold a large share of their savings in bank deposits. Government treasury bills and bonds frequently offer a better return than a same-term deposit, but retail access runs mainly through bank branches, with high minimums and manual paperwork. The opportunity is to make that access digital, cheaper to serve, and therefore available at smaller ticket sizes — distributed through brokerage firms that already hold licences and client relationships.

What we do not claim to know. We do not have a defensible estimate of how many Egyptian retail savers would move money into government paper given an easier route, nor of the price elasticity of that decision. We have therefore built the plan so that it works at a small share of any plausible market, and have not sized the opportunity top-down. 4. Revenue model and unit economics

The margin is taken inside the yield and disclosed to the client. Platform defaults, as configured in the product today:

ComponentBasis pointsTo whom
Custody and operations5Custodian / operations
Broker margin50The licensed broker
Platform margin100Agyal
Total deduction from gross yield155Against a 300 bps cap enforced in software

Margins are agreed per broker and may differ from these defaults; the platform enforces a hard ceiling on the total deduction so that a client's yield cannot be eroded beyond a set limit regardless of commercial agreement.

The unit economic. Agyal revenue ≈ assets on the platform × 1.00% × time held. Every EGP 1 million held for a year earns roughly EGP 10,000. Revenue recurs as clients roll maturing paper into new paper, so a client acquired once contributes for as long as they stay invested.

Revenue recognition. Margin accrues to a platform-fee payable account in the broker's ledger at the point of each fill, and is transferred monthly against an invoice — separately from, and never out of, client money.

Cost to serve. Marginal cost per client is small and mostly variable: identity and screening checks at onboarding, message delivery, and a share of hosting. There is no branch, no retail advertising, and no per-client staff cost in the normal case.

7. Break-even

Because revenue is approximately 1.00% of assets on the platform per year, break-even depends on one number only:

Break-even assets on platform = annual operating cost ÷ 1.00% — that is, one hundred times annual cost. At USD 12,000 a year, that is assets equivalent to about USD 1.2 million, whatever the exchange rate does.

So the business covers its costs at roughly EGP 55–66 million of client assets, or about 550–660 clients holding EGP 100,000 each. That is well within the reach of a single brokerage firm's existing client base — the pilot cohort in the investor materials assumes 500 clients at one broker.

This is the core of the plan's prudence argument. Break-even does not require scale, a second product, or a funding round — it requires one broker relationship working properly. Everything beyond that is growth rather than survival. Equally, because the cost base is small and largely variable, a year in which no broker signs is a year of modest cash cost, not an existential event.

9. Funding the guided phase

A guided phase imposes real obligations: connecting live eKYC and AML providers, FIX certification with partner banks, reporting to the Authority, and making good any client loss caused by platform defect or operational error. The plan meets these from existing resources rather than from revenue that has not yet been earned.

The scale of the commitment is modest and known. Running the business costs about USD 1,000 a month with no draw taken, so a full year of operation through a guided phase — including the year-1 shortfall in section 8 — is on the order of USD 12,000, with a funding gap of roughly USD 9,000 after expected revenue. There is no payroll, lease or minimum-volume contract that could escalate that figure.

Operational governance is documented rather than informal: an operations SOP sets out the full client lifecycle with the checks and second approvals at each step; money movements require maker-checker approval; every data change and sign-in is written to an immutable audit log available to the broker's compliance function and to the Authority on request. Service levels, audit rights, business continuity, data return and exit terms sit in the technology agreement with each broker.

As a single-founder company, governance depends on written process and automated control rather than on separation of duties within Agyal. The separation that matters most — between the party that holds client money and the party that operates the software — exists by design, because those are different companies.

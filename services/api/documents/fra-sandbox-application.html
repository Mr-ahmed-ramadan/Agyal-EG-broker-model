The proposition in one paragraph. Agyal is a white-label, FIX-native platform that enables an FRA-licensed brokerage firm to offer retail investors digital access to Egyptian government treasury bills and bonds under the broker's own brand. The broker remains the licensed party, holds all client money in its own segregated account, and owns the books and records. Agyal provides the software only, and never holds client money or securities. We are seeking the Authority's guidance on how this technology-provider model should be structured and supervised.

Sector targeted, and why this is non-banking financial services

Sector: capital markets — retail distribution technology for fixed income. Sub-sector: securities brokerage. Instruments: Egyptian government treasury bills and bonds.

The regulated activity the platform enables is securities brokerage, which sits squarely within the Authority's perimeter: the client relationship, the securities account, suitability and the custody arrangement are all those of an FRA-licensed brokerage firm. Securities are registered at MCDR, under the Authority's supervision.

Partner banks appear in the model only as liquidity counterparties in an over-the-counter market — the role a primary dealer plays when a broker sources paper. No banking service is offered to the client through the platform: no deposit-taking, no payment service, no lending, no stored value. The client's money sits in the licensed broker's own segregated client account, which is an existing banking relationship of the broker's, not a service the platform provides.

Agyal's own position is likewise a non-banking question: whether a technology provider to FRA-licensed brokers is correctly treated under the Authority's outsourcing framework. That is the central matter on which guidance is sought.

1. Innovative idea or technology

The concept is established internationally but has not been applied to Egyptian retail fixed income in this form. Five elements make it innovative in this market:

  • Retail access to government paper at small ticket sizes. Egyptian retail savers today largely reach treasury bills through bank branches, with high minimums and manual paperwork. Because the cost to serve is software rather than branch staff, the platform is built to serve materially smaller tickets economically.
  • Competitive multi-bank pricing for a retail order. On every order the platform issues a FIX 4.4 quote request to all connected partner banks simultaneously and presents the best price to the client. Retail-size fixed-income orders in this market are not normally subject to competitive quoting.
  • Fully digital onboarding into a securities relationship, without a branch visit: identity verification, AML and PEP screening, risk profiling and suitability, and MCDR unified-code registration, all in the application. Risk profiling, suitability, consent capture and the compliance workflow are built and tested. Identity verification and screening run through defined provider interfaces that are connected to stand-ins today. The capture and retention of identity evidence is deliberately not yet built: the evidential standard is question (b) in section 4, and that answer determines what must be captured and kept.
  • Transparent, itemised pricing. The client is shown the net yield they receive and each deduction separately (custody and operations, broker margin, platform margin), rather than an undisclosed spread embedded in the price.
  • A white-label model that multiplies access. One platform can stand behind many licensed brokers, each under its own brand — so the innovation reaches the market through existing licensed firms rather than requiring a new licensed entrant.

Build state, stated plainly. The platform is functionally complete end to end and covered by an automated test suite exercising the full client lifecycle: onboarding and compliance screening, unified-code and custody linkage, deposits, buying and selling over FIX, settlement, coupons and maturity with tax withholding, withdrawals with maker-checker approval, and a double-entry ledger that nets to zero. Two distinctions matter, and we would rather state them here than have them discovered later. First, several external integrations run against simulators: partner-bank FIX, eKYC, AML screening, MCDR custody confirmations and SMS delivery. Each sits behind a defined provider interface with a working implementation on the other side, exercised on every build, so connecting a live licensed provider is an integration and a contract rather than a redesign. Second, and separately, the capture and retention of identity evidence is not built at all, by choice, pending the Authority's answer on the evidential standard. A detailed capability audit setting out exactly what is live, what is simulated and what is not yet built accompanies this application.

2. Benefit to customers and the market

For customers

  • Lower minimums. A digital cost base makes small subscriptions viable, opening sovereign paper to savers currently priced out of it.
  • Geographic reach. Remote onboarding serves governorates with thin branch coverage, and savers who cannot take a working day to visit a branch.
  • Price transparency. The client sees their net yield and every deduction before committing, with a projection of expected proceeds at maturity and tax shown separately.
  • Designed for first-time investors, in Arabic, with risk disclosure in plain language rather than professional terminology.
  • Protected holdings. Every client is onboarded with full KYC and every security is registered to the client's own unified code at MCDR or the CBE — not pooled in a nominee structure.

For the non-banking financial market

  • New distribution capacity for licensed brokers. Smaller brokerage firms gain a retail fixed-income capability they could not economically build alone, strengthening firms that are under competitive pressure.
  • Broader domestic participation in government funding, consistent with national objectives on savings mobilisation and financial inclusion.
  • A formalisation channel. Savings currently held informally move into regulated, registered, KYC'd holdings.
  • Price discovery. Competitive quoting for retail-size orders puts gentle pressure on retail spreads across the market.
  • Digital transformation of a traditionally manual segment, with a complete electronic audit trail available to the broker's compliance function and to the Authority.

3. Roadmap and vision

Launch is staged deliberately, with each stage gated on evidence rather than time:

Stage 4 · ScaleOn the Authority's confirmation of permanent status, caps lift on an agreed schedule and the platform is offered to additional licensed brokers.

Proposed limits during the guided phases — conservative by design, and adjustable to whatever the Authority prefers: per-client holding [EGP 250,000], per-order [EGP 100,000], aggregate cohort exposure [EGP 10,000,000], duration [9 months]. Scope is restricted to Egyptian government treasury bills and bonds in EGP for resident retail individuals aged 18+; no corporate paper, equities, derivatives, FX, margin, leverage, short selling, discretionary management or advice.

3.2 Regulatory alignment and intended legal framework

The model is built so that the licensed broker, not Agyal, is the regulated party in every client relationship and every trade:

ConcernOwner
Client relationship, KYC/AML decision, suitabilityLicensed broker
Client money (segregated client account at a bank)Licensed broker
Books and recordsLicensed broker, using the platform's ledger
ExecutionPartner bank, on the broker's instruction
Custody and unified codeBroker / custodian via MCDR
Software, hosting, data processing, uptimeAgyal

Agyal never holds client money or securities, and this is enforced in the software itself rather than by policy alone. Every money-moving function is restricted to the broker's own finance and operations roles; the platform-operator role has no access to any of them. Client cash exists only in the broker's segregated bank account, mirrored in a double-entry ledger whose trial balance must net to zero. Securities are registered to each client's own unified code. Agyal is remunerated by the broker under a software and services agreement — never by the end client, and never out of client money.

Intended framework. Our working assumption is that Agyal is properly characterised as an outsourced technology and operations provider to FRA-licensed entities, rather than as an entity requiring its own licence. Confirming that characterisation — and the conditions attaching to it — is the principal reason for this application. Data processing is conducted as processor on the broker's instruction under Law 151/2020, with access minimised, audited, and data residency agreed per broker.

3.4 Global precedent

Retail access to government securities through supervised digital channels is well established internationally. The closest analogue is Brazil's Tesouro Direto, operated by the National Treasury with B3, which distributes federal government bonds to retail investors through licensed brokers and agents — structurally the same division of roles proposed here. Other relevant precedents include RBI Retail Direct in India, which opened direct retail access to government securities; TreasuryDirect in the United States; and DhowCSD in Kenya, which digitised retail access to government paper.

On the technology-provider side, the model of a non-licensed platform operating behind licensed brokers is established in several regulated markets, where brokerage-infrastructure providers supply onboarding, order routing and books-and-records software to licensed firms under outsourcing rules with defined audit, continuity and exit obligations.

Precedents are cited as illustration of the regulatory pattern; specifics should be verified before filing, and we would be glad to prepare a fuller comparative note if that would assist the Authority.

4. Specific areas where guidance is sought

The platform is built; our open questions are regulatory rather than technical. In order of importance to us:

a. The correct status for a technology provider in this model

Is a provider that supplies onboarding workflow, order routing and books-and-records software to FRA-licensed brokers — while never holding client money or securities, never contracting with end clients, and never exercising discretion — correctly treated as an outsourced service provider rather than as an entity requiring its own licence? If registration on the Authority's outsourcing register is the appropriate destination, we would welcome guidance on the criteria, the process, and the conditions that would attach.

b. Evidential standards for fully remote onboarding

What evidence does the Authority expect for opening a retail securities relationship without a branch visit — acceptable identity documents, liveness and face-match thresholds, screening scope, record retention — and is remote onboarding sufficient for MCDR unified-code issuance?

c. Evidencing best execution for OTC fixed income

How should a broker evidence best execution where retail-size orders are priced by competing quotes from several banks? What records should be retained for each decision, and how should exceptions be documented?

d. Disclosure of yield-based pricing

Does itemised disclosure of the deduction from gross yield — shown pre-trade and on the contract note, with each component separately identified — meet the Authority's transparency expectations for retail clients? Is any prescribed form or wording required?

e. Structure of the application and the partner requirement

Because the licensed broker is the regulated party, a live test involving client money would run under the broker's licence. Should this application be made by Agyal alone, by the broker, or jointly — and may guidance be given before a licensed partner is signed, with any live phase conditional on naming one?

f. Proportionate limits

Are the proposed caps on cohort size, per-client holding and aggregate exposure in the range the Authority would consider appropriate, and what reporting would it wish to receive during a guided phase?

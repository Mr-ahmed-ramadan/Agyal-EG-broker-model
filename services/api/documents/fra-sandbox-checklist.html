What the FRA asks for, what already exists, what only you can produce, and what to confirm before filing. Internal working document — not for submission.

The route. This is an application for Regulatory Guidance through the FRA Sandbox, submitted via the portal's Apply page after reviewing the published eligibility criteria. It is a narrative submission against four criteria rather than a heavy document filing — so the main application document carries most of the weight, and everything else is supporting material.

4. Questions to raise with the FRA

Criterion 4 invites exactly this, so most of these belong in the application rather than in a separate conversation. These two are better asked first, because the answers change what you file:

Sandbox guidance, or the outsourcing register? If Agyal is purely a technology provider to a licensed broker, the destination may be registration as an outsourcing provider rather than anything sandbox-related. Ask early — the answer could shorten this whole exercise.
Who is the applicant, and is a partner required up front? A live phase with client money runs under the broker's licence, which argues for a joint filing. If a named licensed broker is a precondition for guidance, the partner becomes the critical path and the application waits behind it.

Also worth confirming: whether the submission must be in Arabic (assume yes and budget for legal translation — these drafts are English); whether any fees apply; expected timelines; and what a successful guidance engagement leads to in practice.

5. Suggested sequence

  1. Re-read the official eligibility criteria page and the Apply form, and map the application document onto its actual fields. Field labels and length limits will differ from these headings.
  2. Ask the two questions in §4 before investing further.
  3. Engage Egyptian counsel experienced in FRA licensing to review criteria 3.2 and 4. Do not file the perimeter statements without this.
  4. Fill the management-strategy section (§3.3) — it is the largest genuine gap and cannot be drafted for you.
  5. Write the business plan with projections.
  6. Gather the corporate pack in §3 in parallel; none of it blocks on anything else.
  7. Select eKYC and AML providers and get indicative terms.
  8. Verify the global-precedent detail in §3.4 before citing it to a regulator.
  9. Translate, assemble, review, submit.

6. Two cautions

The partner dependency does not go away. This model is built so a licensed broker holds client money and is counterparty to every trade, so any live phase needs that broker. Applying for guidance does not route around the problem of reaching brokers — but engaging the Authority is a legitimate way to make broker conversations easier, and that is a sound reason to do it.

Do not overclaim readiness. The criteria explicitly accept solutions still under development, so there is no advantage in dressing up the simulators — and real cost if a regulator later finds the bank connectivity was a simulator at the time of filing. The honest version is strong: a complete, tested platform whose remaining step is connecting live licensed providers under supervision.

Sandbox testing plan, KPIs and exit

Accompanies the FRA sandbox application dossier. Sets out what would be tested, within what limits, measured how, protected by what safeguards, and wound down in what way.

Design principle. Every limit below is deliberately conservative. The purpose of the test is to prove the controls hold and to answer the regulatory questions — not to build a book. Caps are proposed, and we would adopt whatever the FRA prefers.

1. Test objectives

  1. Demonstrate that a retail client can be onboarded fully remotely into a securities relationship, to an evidential standard the FRA accepts.
  2. Demonstrate that retail orders routed to multiple partner banks over FIX produce a documented best-execution decision on every trade.
  3. Demonstrate that client money and client securities remain at all times with the licensed broker and the depository, and never with the technology provider — and that this is evidenced by the ledger and the audit trail.
  4. Demonstrate that itemised yield-based pricing is understood by retail clients and disclosed to the FRA's satisfaction.
  5. Establish the operational error rate, reconciliation accuracy and complaint profile of a digital fixed-income desk at small ticket sizes.

4. Phases

Phase 0 · pre-live — [6 weeks]

Controls assessment, no client money

Live eKYC and AML providers connected and tested on internal subjects. FIX certification completed with each partner bank. The FRA is given access to a demonstration environment and the full audit trail. Policies, client agreements and risk disclosures reviewed. Exit criterion: the FRA is satisfied with the controls and confirms move to live.

Phase 1 · friendly live — [8 weeks]

First [20] clients, staff and close contacts

Real money, real trades, lowest caps. Every order reviewed manually by the broker's dealer in addition to the automated checks. Daily reconciliation. Objective: find operational defects where the affected population is small and informed.

Phase 2 · open cohort — [16 weeks]

Up to the full capped cohort, publicly recruited

Caps as in section 3. Manual order review steps down to sampling once the error rate holds. Full KPI measurement runs through this phase. Objective: measure the model at a realistic mix of client experience levels.

Phase 3 · assessment — [4 weeks]

Report, and decide the destination

Final report to the FRA against every KPI, with the regulatory questions answered from evidence. Agreement on the permanent status of the arrangement — outsourcing registration, licence, or conditions.

6. Consumer safeguards

  • Informed participation. Every client is told in writing, in Arabic, that they are in an FRA sandbox test, what that means, and that participation is voluntary.
  • No lock-in. Clients may sell and withdraw at any time, subject only to normal settlement. Exiting the test never requires the client's consent to anything further.
  • Only government paper. The instrument universe is restricted to Egyptian sovereign issues — no credit risk selection by the client.
  • Hard caps on per-client holding and per-order size, enforced by software at order entry rather than by procedure.
  • Suitability and risk profiling at onboarding, with plain-language disclosure and a pre-trade projection showing expected proceeds and tax.
  • Complaints logged in the platform with the broker's own complaints procedure and escalation to the FRA; the full log is reportable.
  • Remediation commitment. [State the agreed position: the broker and Agyal make good any client loss caused by platform defect or operational error during the test, and how this is funded — insurance, indemnity in the technology agreement, or a set-aside.]

8. Stop conditions

Testing halts immediately, and the FRA is notified the same day, on any of:

  • Any unexplained client-money discrepancy, or a trial balance that does not net to zero.
  • Any confirmed data breach affecting client personal data.
  • An eKYC false accept, or any evidence of identity fraud succeeding at onboarding.
  • Breach of a cohort or exposure cap.
  • Settlement failures above [2%] in any rolling week.
  • Loss of the licensed broker partner, or of the last remaining partner bank.
  • Any FRA instruction to suspend.

9. Exit and transition

The test ends by design, not by surprise. Three outcomes are planned for:

ProceedThe FRA confirms the permanent status of the arrangement. Caps lift on an agreed schedule; clients are notified that the test has concluded and normal service continues under the broker's licence.
ModifySpecific conditions are imposed. Changes are implemented and re-evidenced before caps lift.
DiscontinueOrderly wind-down as below.

Wind-down. No new clients and no new buy orders. Clients are notified in writing with at least [30 days] notice. Each client chooses to hold their paper to maturity, sell, or transfer the holding to another licensed broker — the securities are already registered at MCDR under the client's own unified code, so a transfer does not require the position to be sold. Cash is returned to each client's registered bank account under the broker's existing maker-checker payout process. The ledger, audit trail and client records remain the broker's property and are exported in full; Agyal retains nothing beyond what law requires and certifies deletion. A final reconciliation and closing report goes to the FRA.

Because Agyal never holds client money or securities, discontinuation is a software decommissioning, not an insolvency event for clients. That is the central reason the model is structured this way.
